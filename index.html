A little history

How we learned to count down to Christmas

  1. 1839

    The first Advent wreath

    Hamburg pastor Johann Hinrich Wichern put candles on a cart wheel so the children in his care could count the days to Christmas.

  2. 1843

    Christmas cards arrive

    The first commercial Christmas card was sent in England, and the season of writing and posting began.

  3. 1908

    Printed Advent calendars

    German publisher Gerhard Lang is credited with one of the first printed Advent calendars.

  4. 1950s

    Calendars go mainstream

    After the Second World War, mass-produced Advent calendars spread across Europe and North America.

  5. Today

    Countdowns everywhere

    From phone widgets to paper chains, counting the sleeps is a family tradition around the world.

FAQ

Countdown questions

How do I add a Christmas countdown to my website?

Choose a title, theme and size in the widget maker, copy the embed code and paste it into an HTML block on your website or blog. The countdown runs on its own and updates every second.

Is the Christmas countdown widget free?

Yes. The widget is free for personal blogs, school sites, shops and community pages. It is a small piece of HTML and JavaScript with no tracking.

Does the widget switch to next Christmas automatically?

Yes. It shows a Merry Christmas message on December 25 and starts counting down to the following Christmas from December 26.

How many links should my paper chain have?

One link for every sleep until Christmas. The printable chain fills in today’s number automatically, or you can choose a different number, for example 24 for a December-only chain.

What time zone does the countdown use?

Both the page and the widget count down to midnight on December 25 in the visitor’s own time zone.

Where did the Advent wreath come from?

The Advent wreath is credited to Johann Hinrich Wichern, a Lutheran pastor in Hamburg, who used a wheel with candles in 1839 to help children count the days to Christmas.
